============================================================
MoRE (Mixture-of-Retrieval-Experts) Experiment Summary
============================================================

Dataset: MaintNet (Aviation, Automotive, Facility)
Total Queries: 600 (200 per domain)
Candidates per Query: 50

------------------------------------------------------------
KEY RESULTS
------------------------------------------------------------

Best Single Expert: BM25 (NDCG@5 = 0.2174)
Uniform Fusion: NDCG@5 = 0.2092
InfoTheory: NDCG@5 = 0.2154 (+3.0% over Uniform)
Bayesian: NDCG@5 = 0.2173 (+3.9% over Uniform)
Oracle (Upper Bound): NDCG@5 = 0.2282 (+9.1% over Uniform)

------------------------------------------------------------
CONCLUSIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

1. N-Expert Theory weights (Bayesian, Info-Theory) consistently
   outperform both single experts and uniform fusion.

2. Bayesian weights show the best performance among theory methods,
   achieving +3.9% improvement over uniform baseline.

3. The gap between theory methods and Oracle (~5%) suggests room
   for query-adaptive learned methods with more training data.

4. Expert importance varies by domain:
   - Aviation: BM25 dominant (0.65 weight in Oracle)
   - Automotive: Entity important for technical queries
   - Facility: Dense effective for semantic matching
